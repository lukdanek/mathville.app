Why it helps

Mathville was made for a child who found math practice hard going. Everything in it serves one aim: plenty of practice that feels like play, without stress.

It starts where your child is

Four ladders of small steps: adding and taking away, times tables, division, and missing numbers. After a run of right answers the game moves up a step; after a few misses it quietly steps back, so most answers stay right. It starts easy, on purpose.

No pressure

There is no clock unless a grown-up turns one on: a quick answer only earns a stronger blow. A wrong answer never shows the hero failing. Mistakes are part of playing, not a punishment.

Practice that doesn't feel like homework

Every building, upgrade, hunt, fight and trade is a small task, and there is always something new to build. Our first player solved 738 problems in nine days.

Parents see how it's going

Behind a grown-up check you can set each ladder and the clock by hand, and see the last two weeks, the times tables fact by fact, the latest mistakes and a tip on what to practice next.

Made at home, with my son

Mathville began in October 2026 with one player in mind: my son, in third grade, for whom practising sums felt like a chore. I wanted math he would ask to play.

The first version missed. It started him on sums past a hundred with a clock ticking, and within hours it was stressing him out. We rebuilt it that same day: now it starts easy, climbs in small steps only when he is ready, and the clock stays off unless a parent turns it on. He has shaped the game ever since. When the music distracted him, it became quieter and now plays only in the village.

It worked. The cards show the kind of problems he was solving when he started, and nine days later.

I didn't build it alone, either. Mathville was made together with Claude, an AI from Anthropic. I described what we needed and played it with my son every evening; Claude wrote the code, built the 3D world from simple shapes, made the sound effects and translated everything into five languages. Often his feedback from one evening was in the game the next day.

How to play

  1. Start a game

    Type a name and choose a hero. Up to six players can each keep their own game.

  2. Build the village

    Tap a building to build or upgrade it. Each one costs coins and a few sums.

  3. Go on expeditions

    Hunt, fight, dig for stones and trade along the trail.

  4. Finish the quests

    Eleven quests lead up to the Trial of the Age.

  5. Reach a new age

    Beat the boss and the island moves on, all the way to the Future.
